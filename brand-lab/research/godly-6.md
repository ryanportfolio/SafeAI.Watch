## Patterns

1. **Letter substitution inside an otherwise plain wordmark**: one glyph (o, i-dot, h) is swapped for a small image, leaving the word fully legible. Examples: Afternoon, Healthpoint, Shh.
2. **Negative space or counter that carries the concept**: the hole in a letter or ring is the meaning. Examples: Pengdeck, Cluely, Deeliver.
3. **Continuous or single-weight line symbols**: one stroke width, closed loops, no fill. Examples: Polymarket, Manifold, A Globe.
4. **Symmetric radial or modular constructions**: one module repeated around a centre. Examples: Multiply, 12/28, Deeliver.
5. **Dot-matrix and pixel type as motion vehicle**: grid of dots or pixels that animate to form letters. Examples: Wasabi, Miraweb.
6. **Gradient, glow and photo-background presentation**: marks shown over blurred gradients, aurora, landscapes and topographic photography to imply depth; the mark itself is usually flat. Examples: Healthpoint, Stratus, Converge, Optimus, Miso.
7. **Folded-ribbon and isometric depth with soft gradient**: the most common 3D tell in the slice. Examples: Mask, Fuse, Flooben.
8. **Stencil and cut-out geometry**: letters defined by gaps, notches and offset bars. Examples: Gravity, Ecstron, Sybil.
9. **Friendly blob mascots with eyes**: rounded creature marks for consumer apps. Examples: Ghost, Apptics, Flooben, Macros. A trend to avoid for a serious editorial brand.
10. **Serif wordmarks with one flourish for trust**: editorial serif replacing grotesque for health, finance and lifestyle. Examples: Afternoon, Healthpoint, Optimus.

## Ideas for SafeAI.watch

1. **Ring with one gap (the held breath)**: a thin ring in mark blue with a single small cut at the top; the cut is the point where attention sits, and the same gap can rotate slowly as the "watch" motion. Borrows negative-space cut (Cluely-type vocabulary, redrawn from scratch) and line-weight discipline.
2. **Dot replaced by a reticle in ".watch"**: set the wordmark in Newsreader lowercase, and swap the period before "watch" for a small concentric-circle dot (the old crosshair reduced to one glyph). One glyph swap, fully legible, links to the current mark. Borrows letter substitution (Afternoon, Healthpoint).
3. **Two halves, one shape**: a square or circle split by a single diagonal into an ink half and an orange half that lock together as one form, meaning benefit and danger from the same capability. Borrows the interlocking-halves construction (Ecstron) and two-tone flatness.
4. **Dot field that focuses**: a grid of small identical dots that resolves into a single ring or the letters "SAW" when the page loads, then relaxes into a static 3x3 favicon. Borrows the dot-matrix motion idea (Wasabi); meaning is noise resolving into a signal.
5. **Redaction bar monogram**: an "S" cut from a solid bar by two horizontal gaps, like a redacted line on a document; gaps let paper colour through. Meaning is sourcing and what is withheld; borrows stencil cut-outs (Gravity, Sybil).
6. **Hatched lens**: a disc filled with fine parallel lines whose density steps from sparse to dense across the diameter, read as evidence strength from uncertain to established. Borrows line-in-disc (Stratus) and engraving hatch (Xactly) in a flat single ink colour.
