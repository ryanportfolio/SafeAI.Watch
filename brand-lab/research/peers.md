# Research: peers

Slice: marks of organizations SafeAI.watch sits near (AI safety and evaluation bodies, trusted editorial and evidence brands, "watch" names). Checked live on 2026-09-30 by headless Chrome: header logo element screenshotted at 2x to 3x, SVG or image URL read from the DOM. Nothing here is from godly.design.

Headline finding: no peer in this set uses a crosshair, brain, or circuit motif. The crowded motifs are eyes and magnifiers (the "watch" names), globes and rings (institutes), and open-A "AI" monograms. Editorial brands mostly carry no symbol at all; they win on one typographic move.

## Patterns

1. **Symbol-free editorial wordmarks.** ProPublica, The Markup, Our World in Data, Bellingcat, Human Rights Watch. Trust comes from type and restraint.
2. **One glyph replaced by an idea.** Bellingcat (i as an inverted question mark), Retraction Watch (o as a lens with an eye), Apollo Research (O as a segmented ring), Future of Life (flame inside the serif lowercase).
3. **Abstract symbol plus wordmark in AI safety labs.** METR (gauge), Epoch AI (three slanted bars), FAR.AI (wedge), Center for AI Safety (aperture swirl), Redwood (tree in ring). Typical forms: rounded monoline or geometric solid, 1 to 2 colors.
4. **Open-A "AI" monograms and custom geometric caps.** UK AISI, AI Incident Database, AI Now, FAR.AI. Four peers already own "an A without a crossbar".
5. **Two-part names shown with a fill or weight contrast.** Climate Watch (bold then light), AI Now (outline then solid), Center for AI Safety (roman then italic "for").
6. **Institutional marks are borrowed, not designed.** NIST's logo stands alone for the US center (it is now CAISSI, Center for Advancing Innovation and Standards for Super Intelligence, at nist.gov/caissi; its header shows only the NIST mark and a green band). The EU AI Office and EU AI Watch both use the European Commission building-and-flag logo. The International AI Safety Report header is a plain text title over a purple geometric banner.
7. **Literal "watch" imagery.** Retraction Watch (eye in lens), CorpWatch (eye with globe), Food & Water Watch (magnifier around WATCH). Where the name is enough, marks skip it: Human Rights Watch, AlgorithmWatch, Climate Watch, AI Lab Watch (plain text heading on its site, now unmaintained since September 2025).
8. **Palette.** Navy and cobalt with white dominate (European Commission, HRW, Our World in Data, AIID, CAIS, Stanford HAI, Partnership on AI, Consumer Watchdog). Outliers use one saturated hue: pink (Markup), cyan (Full Fact), yellow (Snopes), red (AISI), teal (Epoch), green (Redwood). No peer combines a warm paper ground with orange; that space is open.
9. **Bars and stripes.** Epoch AI, Full Fact, Stanford HAI (stripes through HAI), European Commission (striped building).
10. **Circles as containers.** Redwood (open ring), Apollo (segmented O), OECD.AI (figure in a circle), IWF (meridian globe), CSET (globe with nodes).

## Ideas for SafeAI.watch

1. **Slit S.** A Newsreader-derived lowercase or capital S cut by one hairline horizontal gap (the viewing slit or a horizon line). Meaning: observing through a narrow opening, calm and exact. Borrows the glyph-swap method (Apollo, Bellingcat) without any eye or ring. Risk: the slit reads as a glitch at small sizes; test at 16 px.
2. **Three-register stack.** A square split into three rows drawn in three line styles: solid fill, hatched, dashed outline, matching the entry structure (what happened, what the evidence shows, what remains uncertain). Vary by line style, not by slanted bars, so it stays away from Epoch and Full Fact. Works as a static mark or as an animated reveal of the rows.
3. **Orange point after a serif name.** Wordmark "SafeAI" in ink Newsreader, then a single orange round dot, then "watch" smaller in Geist Mono. The dot is the only color and the only symbol; it marks the point where you stop and look. Borrows Future of Life's one-colored-element idea and the name's own structure. FAR.AI proves a dot alone is ordinary, so the dot must carry a precise size and position rule (for example, the dot's diameter equals the x-height stem).
4. **Double exposure S.** A geometric S drawn twice, offset by a few pixels, one copy ink and one orange, with multiply overlap giving a third tone. Meaning: benefit and risk from the same shape. Technique: overlap and offset on a geometric letterform, not a yin-yang circle.
5. **Threshold line.** A single ink hairline baseline with one small right-angled step up, then back (a recorded event on a calm line), used as the symbol beside the wordmark and animated as a tracker sweeping left to right and logging the step. Borrows the monoline symbol approach of Epoch and the instrument idea of METR without a gauge or pulse spike. Watch out for ECG or heartbeat look; keep the step right-angled.
6. **Corner citation mark.** One small L-shaped corner bracket placed before the wordmark, reading as a crop mark and a footnote anchor (every entry links its source). One corner, not a pair around the name, so it stays clear of CHT's brackets.
