# Facts for the SafeAI.watch About film

This file merges the ten recon slices in `film/recon/` (risk-incidents, risk-evals, warnings, progress-research, policy, site-record, site-look; gaps, added in a second pass; benefits and harris, added in a third) into one checked set of facts for the short, silent animation that will sit on the site's About page and cover AI safety as a whole: where AI is going wrong or could go wrong, and where safety work is succeeding. The owner's direction binds every use of it. The tone is calm and focused, with no doom, alarm or hype. Risk and progress get equal weight and the same evidence standard, with no inflated wins and no inflated harms, and the film holds both at once: the same capabilities bring measured benefit and real danger. Newest sources are preferred (June to September 2026); older ones appear only where they are still the latest word or foundational, and their dates must be shown. The film should stay true for years, so `durable` facts win over `perishable` ones when both fit. Directors and critics use nothing outside this file: every fact, number, quote, color and still in the film comes from a row here, each Exact text cell is verbatim from its source and must never be reworded, and each row's Limits travel with it. Compiled 2026-09-30 from the slice files only; nothing was re-fetched for this merge.

## Facts

How to read this table:

- One table, grouped by ID letter: H happened, T shown-in-test, W warning, P progress, L policy, B benefit (a real, measured good AI has done for people), Q Tristan Harris (every Harris row, whatever its type; most are `perspective`, a line about how to understand or weigh the tradeoff), S site. IDs are stable. The first pass numbered each group newest first; rows added or moved in the second pass continue the sequence, newest first among themselves. S rows keep the site-record order.
- Second-pass changes: the gaps slice added rows at the end of each group, and 12 evaluation incidents moved from `shown-in-test` back to `happened` with new IDs (T15 is now H37; T30 is now H47; T25 is now H46; T16 is now H38; T17 is now H39; T23 is now H44; T24 is now H45; T6 is now H36; T19 is now H40; T20 is now H41; T21 is now H42; T22 is now H43). The old T numbers are retired and not reused.
- Third-pass changes: benefits rows keep the slice's own numbers B1 to B23; the slice's B21 is a test result and became T73, so B21 is unused. Harris rows are Q1 to Q17 in the slice's order; their Limits keep each timestamp and context line.
- Every row was judged ACCURATE by its slice. Site-record rows are accurate to the repo at `e66dded`; the record's underlying claims were not re-fetched.
- Record entries keep the evidence type the site-record slice gave them from their content, so they sit among the web rows. S rows are the site's own copy and record data (count, dates, domain).
- Evaluation incidents: if an event's effects reached real people, real systems or real public infrastructure outside the evaluation sandbox (an agent acting against real people, code submitted to a real public repository, a real company's systems breached), it is `happened`, and its Limits say it occurred during an evaluation. If it stayed inside a controlled test environment, it is `shown-in-test`.
- Where two slices held the same event, the row keeps the stronger source and the fuller Limits and names both slices. Record entries that repeat a web row are merged into it with `Also in record` in Limits.
- Durability is set only on rows used in `## Hooks`; `-` elsewhere.
- Source is the original URL; record rows add the entry number and its `events.json` line.

### Look notes (verbatim from site-look)

- Ground is warm paper `#d7d7d0` with warm near-black ink `#1a1614` and cream `#f4f4e7`. Hierarchy comes from ink alpha (0.7, 0.64, 0.34, 0.1), not extra colors. A film on paper keeps this ground; a dark passage uses the cool-to-warm dark grounds (`#161f29`, `#080c10`, `#181a15`) with grain, never flat black or white.
- Accent set is small and fixed: orange `#ff7733`, amber `#e5a700`, olive `#a89a1a`, plus ink for the four hero categories; blue `#6a86c2`/`#253e77` is rare. Keep to these; room to go further is in how they combine (for example olive for progress, orange for the one thing being followed), not in new hues.
- Type: Newsreader serif at weight 400 with tight negative tracking for anything read as a statement, Geist for UI and prose, Geist Mono 9-11px uppercase with 0.1em tracking for labels. Italic Newsreader has lowercase letters only (`abcdefghijklmnopqrstuvwxyz .,;:-’`); a capital or digit in italic will fall back. Film titles in italic must be lowercase or the subset must be rebuilt.
- Shape language is thin: 1px lines, round dots (sizes about 1-14px), dashed segments, dashed outlines with solid corner ticks, translucent planes, soft gaussian glows, small mono chips joined to nodes by dashed leaders. No text in WebGL, no gradients on objects, no icons, no meshes. Room to go further: longer choreography than the site's 12s loops, camera moves, and more than one scene per story beat, using the same vocabulary.
- Motion is slow and eased: 4-6s holds, 1.25-1.5s morphs on cubic ease-in-out, exponential-smoothed follow, ambient pulses every 1.9s, 24 fps film grain on dark. Nothing snaps or bounces. A film can be denser in time (it has a timeline and a script) but should keep the same softness and the "scatter, then put in order" gesture the hero already uses.
- Site imagery is conceptual and carries no data (project rule). The film may not draw invented curves or counts; anything with a number needs its own source.
- For build: no seek or fixed-step exists in `runtime.ts`; scenes accept `render(time, still)` so a film harness can import them and drive time itself. WebGL2 on the real GPU renders these fine in headed Chrome (stills above).
