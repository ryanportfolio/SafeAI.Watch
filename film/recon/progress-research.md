## Hook candidates

Durable hooks first.

1. `durable`. "Progress on software security used to be limited by how quickly we could find new vulnerabilities. Now it's limited by how quickly we can verify, disclose, and patch..." (Anthropic, 2026-05-22). A lasting pattern (finding outpaces fixing), a win and its limit in one calm sentence. Use the sentence, not the counts; the counts (2,300 disclosed, 421 patched, contested) go stale.
2. `durable`. "Claude then reports that the sport it was thinking of is rugby." (Anthropic, 2026-07-06). Concrete and visual, no alarm: an internal pattern is swapped and the answer follows. Stands in for interpretability as a way to read a model. The method outlives one model, though the demo is on Claude.
3. `durable`. UK AISI: "We found vulnerabilities in every monitor version we tested, driving an iterative improvement cycle." (2026-07-23). Government testers breaking a lab's monitor until it improves; oversight of the overseers. Independent, and the pattern holds for any future monitor.
4. `perishable`. "the models never engage in blackmail, where previous models would sometimes do so up to 96% of the time (Opus 4)" (Anthropic, 2026-05-08). Clean before and after number, but tied to named model versions and one eval that may saturate or leak into training data. Must carry the "fictional scenario, own eval, models may know it is a test" caveat. Durable variant from the same post: training on examples that show reasoning about values beat training on matching behavior (22% to 15% versus 3%).
5. `perishable`. Mozilla: 423 security bugs fixed in April, 180 of the AI-found 271 rated sec-high (2026-05-07). Reporter is the maintainer, not the lab, and Mozilla says a high bug is not a working exploit. Tied to one model and one month.
6. `perishable`. Independent jailbreak result (arXiv, 2026-08-04): two models held against every attack while the same paper found hundreds of universal jailbreaks on two others, a "hundredfold difference". Leaderboard number, rolling, dated by model versions.
