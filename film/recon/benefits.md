# Recon: benefits

Slice `benefits`. Compiled 2026-09-30. Follows `film/recon/RULES.md`, including Timelessness. Owner's thesis for this slice: hold both at once. The same AI capabilities bring measured benefit and real danger, and people should use and value these tools while understanding the risks. Same evidence standard as the risk slices: measured outcomes only, no hype, each source's own limits kept.

- `film/facts.md` IDs in Pairings were read from that file as merged on 2026-09-30; nothing there was re-fetched.

## Hook candidates

1. **B4, Nobel Prize in Chemistry 2024 (AlphaFold2).** Durable. Award citation from the prize body: virtually all 200 million known proteins predicted, used by more than two million people in 190 countries. Calm, checkable, and it ties to the biology risk story below.
2. **B18, AFB survey.** Durable pattern. 51% of blind or low-vision users of AI description use it daily, and 21% report harm from an error. One source, one group of people, both sides of the thesis.
3. **B12, MASAI trial plus its published critique.** Durable. A randomized trial of 105,934 women found higher sensitivity at equal specificity, and the same journal printed a warning about overdiagnosis. The evidence standard shown on screen.
4. **B22, DARPA AIxCC.** Durable. Government-run contest: 54 planted flaws found, 43 patched, 18 real ones found. Defense and attack use the same skill.
5. **B19 with B21, tutor versus unguarded chatbot.** Durable. The same tool helps in a guarded design and lowered exam scores by 17% without guardrails. Two peer-reviewed results, one capability.
6. **B1, Pew 2026.** Perishable. About half of U.S. adults use chatbots; more adults expect AI to hurt than help. Use and unease at once, from an independent survey.

## Pairings

Each pair is one capability that carries both a benefit (row in this table) and a risk (row in `film/facts.md`). The pairing is this slice's reading; each source stands on its own. "Loose" means the capability is shared but no facts.md row names the same mechanism.

1. **Finding software flaws (defense and attack).** Benefit: B22, B23; also already in facts.md as P5 (Anthropic dashboard, 2,300 disclosed, 421 patched), P11 (Gemini 3.5 Flash Cyber on V8), P18 (OpenAI and Trail of Bits patch work), P24 (Mozilla, 423 Firefox security bugs). Risk: H8 (AI-driven vulnerability research by an attacker), H23 (first AI-developed zero-day exploit in criminal hands), H31 (state-sponsored automated espionage), H15, T9, T13, T62 (rising offensive cyber skill). Google's B23 and H23 come from the same threat-intelligence team. This is the cleanest pair in the set.
2. **Biology models (medicine and misuse).** Benefit: B4 (AlphaFold2 and protein design), B10 (Co-Scientist, leukaemia candidates in cells), B14 (rentosertib). Risk: H13 (Anthropic's five cases of possible biological misuse), T5 (SecureBio: frontier score above best expert virologist on a troubleshooting benchmark), W36 (Nature feature on AI-designed bioweapons). Safeguard side: P13 (screening tools patched after AI-designed evasions), W34 (letter on DNA order screening). Counterweight: T50 (pre-registered trial, no significant end-to-end lab uplift for novices with mid-2025 models).
3. **Fluent tutoring and answering.** Benefit: B19, B20. Cost: B21 (same capability, guardrails decide). Risk: H1 (2,095 court decisions with AI-hallucinated content), H32 (lawsuits alleging GPT-4o harmed users), H35 (rollback of a flattering GPT-4o update).
4. **Describing images, speech and records.** Benefit: B18 (visual description and captions), B16 (ambient scribes). Risk: the same errors. B18's own 21% of blind or low-vision users harmed by mistakes; B16's "occasional inaccuracies"; H1 and H2 (a chatbot misidentified cargo on a Chinese ship before officials caught it).
5. **Autonomous long-horizon reasoning.** Benefit: B8 (IMO gold-level), B9 (Erdős problem), B10. Risk: H16 (OpenAI agent swarm and the Navier-Stokes statement, same capability), T37 and T41 (METR measures of long-task agents), T13 and T9 (the same reasoning shows up in cyber ratings). Loose beyond H16.
6. **Voice synthesis.** Benefit: B17 (a speech neuroprosthesis speaks in the participant's pre-ALS voice). Risk: AI-enabled fraud, H27 (FBI: 22,364 AI-related complaints, nearly $893 million) and H12. Loose: facts.md has no voice-cloning row.
7. **Mass adoption itself.** Benefit: B1, B2, B3 (use at the scale of billions of weekly sessions and about half of U.S. adults). Risk: the same reach carries every failure in pairs 3 and 4; Pew's finding that more adults expect AI to hurt than help them is the public's own version of hold-both.
8. **No clean risk pair found for weather forecasting (B5 to B7) or the FDA device count (B15).** Those rows are benefit only in this evidence base. B12's overdiagnosis critique is the closest, and it is a limit on the benefit rather than a separate risk.

## Rejected

- **ChatGPT "1.2 billion weekly users" as reported by FourWeekMBA and similar sites.** Replaced by the provider's own recap (B2). Secondary write-ups and search snippets are leads only.
- **Be My Eyes "1 million blind and low-vision users, 10 million volunteers" (2026-03-12, company press release, https://www.bemyeyes.com/news/be-my-eyes-reaches-1-million-blind-and-low-vision-users-and-10-million-volunteers/).** MISLEADING as an AI benefit: it counts the whole platform, which is mainly live volunteer video calls; the release gives no AI-specific usage or outcome figure. Company marketing. Dropped in favor of B18.
- **"AI beat the National Hurricane Center."** OVERSTATED. NHC's own abstract says official forecasts "generally outperformed most dynamical and consensus models" with the AI model slightly better at short range (B5).
- **"AI-supported mammography cut interval cancers by 12%."** MISLEADING as a headline. The trial's primary result was non-inferiority with a ratio of 0·88 whose interval (0·65–1·18) includes 1 (B12).
- **"AI scribes reduce physician burnout."** OVERSTATED. Burnout results were secondary endpoints the authors say need confirmation (B16).
- **"GNoME discovered 2.2 million new materials."** OVERSTATED. Predicted crystal structures; independent reviewers found "scant evidence" of novelty, credibility and utility in what they sampled (B11).
- **"First AI-discovered drug approved."** WRONG. No regulator has approved rentosertib; phase III reported started (B14).
- **Takeda zasocitinib as an AI-discovered drug.** UNVERIFIED. One secondary source (from search only); its origin is described there as physics-based modeling and it was not fetched.
- **OpenAI's IMO 2025 result as officially graded.** UNVERIFIED. Only Google's post was fetched; the search summary says OpenAI graded its own result.
- **Other 2026 AI-solved Erdős problems (#1148, #1196, #1202 and others) from aggregator posts.** UNVERIFIED. Not fetched; #728 is the only one read at source (B9).
- **AISLE "12 of 12 OpenSSL zero-days" and Palo Alto Unit 42 "14,090 confirmed vulnerabilities."** UNVERIFIED. Vendor claims seen only in search results; not fetched.
- **Anthropic CVD dashboard (2,300 disclosed, 421 patched) and OpenAI Patch the Planet.** Both fetched and ACCURATE, but already merged into facts.md (P5, P18); not repeated here. facts.md P5 records that VulnCheck disputes the 421 count.
- **Rentosertib phase III "first dosing on September 9, 2026."** UNVERIFIED. Search sources disagree on the start date; only the 2026-07-08 Drug Target Review report was fetched.
