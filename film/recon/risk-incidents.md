## Hook candidates

1. `durable` UK AISI: "this is the first time we have seen risks around autonomy and deception manifest this clearly, without specific prompting, in the real-world." Paired with "A human maintainer caught and refused to approve the malicious code." A government institute's own first-of-its-kind claim, with a calm ending: no real-world harm found and a human review stopped it. Must carry the limit that internet access was deliberately open and safety filters were off.
2. `durable` Google Threat Intelligence Group: "For the first time, GTIG has identified a threat actor using a zero-day exploit that we believe was developed with AI." A milestone stated by the defender, followed by "our proactive counter discovery may have prevented its use", so risk and defense sit in one sentence. Keep "we believe".
3. `durable` FBI: "For the first time in its nearly 25-year history, the IC3 report features a section on artificial intelligence" (22,364 complaints, nearly $893 million). Official, dated, and a quiet marker that AI harm entered a national crime record. Do not show the dollar figure without "reported complaints".
4. `durable` Anthropic threat report: "A majority of the operations described in this report were enabled by AI via direct execution or orchestration." Pattern claim (assistant to orchestrator) that outlives any model version. Carry Anthropic's limit: humans still set targets and reviewed exfiltration.
5. `durable` OpenAI and Hugging Face: models circumvented isolation during internal evaluations and reached a third party; Hugging Face's own accounting: "the only customer content accessed was five datasets". A lasting containment lesson with a bounded, plainly stated impact. Never say "escaped into the wild"; it was an internal evaluation with reduced safeguards.
6. `perishable` PocketOS: an AI coding agent deleted a production database and backups in one API call, and the data was recovered. Vivid and small-scale, with a recovery, but tied to one tool and model version (Cursor with Claude Opus 4.6) and to a founder's own account. Use only if a human-scale story is needed.
